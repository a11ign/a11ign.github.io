Why a11ign exists

The gap we saw

Automated checks read your code. They can’t hear your page. Deque reports that axe-core finds about 57% of WCAG issues automatically and leaves the rest for a person to review. Much of that rest is what it’s really like to use a screen reader.

Testing with a real screen reader is the answer, but it’s slow, Windows-only and hard to repeat, so most teams skip it. We built a11ign to make it something you can run.

What we believe

  • Proof, not scores

    Every finding quotes what the screen reader said. There’s no score or badge, because one number hides what was not checked.

  • Clear scope first

    We say what a11ign covers, and what we’re still proving, up front.

  • Nothing leaves your machine

    No tracking and no phoning home. If that ever changes, it will be opt-in and arrive in a major version.

  • Open by licence

    Licensed AGPL-3.0-or-later. Anyone who runs a modified version as a service has to share their changes.

How it’s built

  • A testing pipeline

    Drives real NVDA through a page, checks what it said, and reports findings against WCAG.

  • Repeatable setup

    A scripted Windows build and GitHub-hosted runners mean anyone can run it, the same way each time.

  • Our own model

    Trained on what the first two produce. It flags findings for a person to judge. It doesn’t decide them.

What’s next

a11ign is live and working today. It’s a new kind of tool, and we’re only getting started.

  • Accessibility specialists labelling real pages, so we can check our calls against theirs.
  • More screen readers, starting with JAWS and VoiceOver.
  • Multi-step flows, like signing in and checking out.
  • The quick check, running inside the Playwright tests you already have.
