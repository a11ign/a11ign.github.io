Why a11ign exists

Most accessibility checks read your code. We listen to your page.

The gap we saw

57%

of WCAG issues are found automatically by axe-core, according to Deque.

Automated checks read your code. They can’t hear your page. The rest is left for a person to review, and much of it is what it’s really like to use a screen reader.

Testing with a real screen reader is the answer, but it’s slow, Windows-only and hard to repeat, so most teams skip it. We built a11ign to change that: real NVDA, on a machine GitHub provides, with the proof quoted on your pull request.

What we believe

  • Proof, not scores

    Every finding quotes what the screen reader said. There’s no score or badge, because one number hides what was not checked.

  • Clear scope first

    We say what a11ign covers, and what’s still to come, up front.

  • No data sent to us

    We never see your code, and there’s no tracking or phoning home. If that ever changes, it will be opt-in and arrive in a major version.

  • Open by licence

    Licensed AGPL-3.0-or-later. Anyone who runs a modified version as a service has to share their changes.

How it’s built

  • A testing pipeline

    Drives real NVDA through a page, checks what it said, and reports findings against WCAG.

  • Repeatable setup

    A scripted Windows build and GitHub-hosted runners mean anyone can run it, the same way each time.

  • Our own model

    Trained on what the first two produce. It flags findings for a person to judge. It doesn’t decide them.

Where we’re going

a11ign is live and working today. Here’s what we’re building next.

  • Coming soon

    A screen reader’s view on every commit

    A quick check that runs inside the Playwright tests you already have. No Windows machine and no extra pipeline.

  • Planned

    More screen readers

    Test with the ones your users actually use. JAWS and VoiceOver are next, then Orca.

  • Planned

    Whole journeys, not just pages

    Sign up, search, check out. Playwright drives the page while the screen reader listens to every step.

  • Planned

    Catch the wrong voice

    Hear when a page is read out in the wrong language, or when a button’s announced name doesn’t match what’s on screen.
